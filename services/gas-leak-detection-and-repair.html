The gas lines here are the same age as the water lines

North La Puente and its sibling tracts from the 1950s and 1960s were plumbed with steel gas pipe the same era as the copper water lines now sixty to seventy years deep into their statistical failure window. Steel corrodes on its own schedule in this alluvial soil, appliance connectors outlive their rated service life unnoticed, and the slow seismic flexing common to this part of the San Gabriel Valley works loose the same joints and fittings that stress water lines. A gas leak rarely announces itself the way a water leak does — no puddle, no climbing bill. Usually it is a faint rotten-egg smell near a meter or appliance, a hissing sound, or dead vegetation over a buried line.

There is no visible sign for most gas leaks until someone notices the smell. Trust your nose first, and call before investigating further yourself.

What to do first if you smell gas

Get everyone out of the house. Do not flip a light switch, use a phone indoors, or search for the source with a lighter or match. If you can reach the meter without lingering, shut the gas off with a wrench — a quarter turn so the valve sits perpendicular to the pipe. Call SoCalGas from outside, then call (626) 898-6169. We coordinate with the utility rather than around it, since only SoCalGas can restore service to the meter once a repair is complete.

How we locate the leak

Once the utility confirms it is safe to proceed, we pressure-test the line in sections and use a combustible gas detector along every joint, union, and appliance connection rather than relying on smell alone. Buried yard lines get traced with the same acoustic and tracer-gas methods we use on buried water leaks, marking the failure point instead of trenching the whole run blind.

Where gas leaks most often start here

Flexible appliance connectors to a stove, dryer, or water heater have a service life, and a common failure point is a fixture swapped with the old connector reused. Meter sets on 1950s-era tract homes sometimes carry thread sealant never rated for current line pressure. Buried steel service lines corrode in the alluvial soil the same way anywhere else, showing up as a smell in the yard or dead grass in a straight line. Repairs beyond a simple connector swap are permitted work, and we pull those permits so the fix is documented and inspected.

Gas leak questions from La Puente homeowners

Is it safe to stay inside if I smell a little gas?

No. Leave immediately and call from outside. A faint smell can concentrate quickly in an enclosed space, and there is no reliable way to judge severity by nose alone.

Will SoCalGas find the leak for me?

SoCalGas responds to reported leaks and can shut off service if needed, but locating the exact failure point and completing the repair is licensed plumbing work, which is where we come in.

How much does gas leak repair cost?

It depends entirely on where the leak is. An appliance connector swap is usually a same-visit fix; a buried service line replacement is a bigger job. We quote in writing after locating the leak, before repair starts.
