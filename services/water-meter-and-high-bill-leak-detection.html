The leaks that move your bill most are the ones you cannot see

A water bill that climbs with no visible leak is one of the most common reasons people call us in La Puente. A slab leak stays under the floor. A silently leaking toilet flapper runs into the bowl and down the drain, invisible unless you check for it specifically. Your water meter cannot be fooled the same way — it registers every gallon passing through whether or not you can see where that water went, making it the fastest, cheapest diagnostic step before we ever send a truck.

Shut every fixture off, watch the meter for fifteen minutes. If the sweep hand or flow indicator still moves, something in the system is open.

How to run the meter test yourself, right now

Turn off every fixture and appliance that uses water, including anything on an automatic cycle like an ice maker or irrigation timer. Find your meter, note the reading, wait at least two hours without using any water, then read it again. Any movement means water left the system somewhere between the meter and your fixtures. Common causes behind a high bill with no visible leak

Silent toilet leaks. A worn flapper lets water seep from tank to bowl continuously; food coloring in the tank showing up in the bowl within ten minutes confirms it. Slab leaks. Water escaping under the foundation shows on the bill before it shows as a warm floor patch — see our slab leak page. Water service line leaks. The buried line between the meter and the house can leak into surrounding soil without ever surfacing. Once you have confirmed loss with the meter test, we combine that flow-rate data with acoustic listening, pressure isolation, and thermal imaging to pinpoint exactly where the water is going.

High water bill questions from La Puente homeowners

My bill has been high for months, not just one cycle. Does that matter?

It usually points to a smaller, slower leak rather than a sudden failure, since a large leak tends to show up dramatically within one cycle. Slow leaks are exactly what the meter test is built to find.

Could this just be a meter reading error?

It happens, but rarely. If your own meter test confirms flow with everything off, that rules out a reading error and confirms real water loss somewhere in your system.

How much water does a running toilet actually waste?

A worn flapper can waste 200 gallons a day or more, silently, which alone is enough to visibly move a monthly bill.
